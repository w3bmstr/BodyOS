What's happening hour by hour
0–12 hours
Digestion → glycogen depletion
mTOR active, autophagy fully suppressed. Insulin falls progressively after hour 4. Body in storage and build mode.
12–16 hours
Ketosis begins — AMPK rises
AMPK (cellular energy sensor) activates, phosphorylates ULK1, and begins switching on autophagy gene expression. Glycogen is largely depleted; ketones rise. DNA repair enzymes start mobilising.
16–18 hours
Autophagy reaches meaningful threshold
ULK1 complex and Beclin-1/VPS34 fully engaged. Phagophores form, LC3-II lipidation rises, and cells begin sequestering damaged proteins, dysfunctional mitochondria and aggregates. This is the daily target window.
18–24 hours
Peak autophagy + immune cell renewal
Peak macroautophagy flux and lysosomal recycling. TFEB-driven gene programs active. Immune cell pruning and renewal underway. Most people break the fast before or around 24 h unless under clinical supervision.
What breaks autophagy: Any calories. Black coffee, plain water, unsweetened ginger tea = fine. Food, juice, or milk → mTOR reactivates within 30 minutes.

The Science
Core mechanisms — why this protocol works
Not medical advice. This page reflects general biology and personal research, not a diagnosis or treatment plan. It is not a substitute for care from a qualified doctor — talk to one before making changes based on anything here.
Master switch
mTOR — growth activator, autophagy suppressor
Activated by food, insulin, and sugar. When mTOR is ON, autophagy is OFF and cellular growth is ON. Turned OFF by fasting, exercise, green tea EGCG, caloric restriction, and some compounds like berberine and metformin. This is the primary switch you're toggling every time you complete the fast window.
Energy sensor
AMPK — the autophagy activator
Activated when cellular energy (ATP) is low — during fasting and exercise. AMPK directly phosphorylates and activates ULK1, the initiating kinase of autophagy. Also activated by ginger compounds (6-gingerol), EGCG, berberine, and resveratrol at food-achievable doses for autophagy support.
Cellular cleanup
Autophagy — digesting the damaged
Cells form double-membrane vesicles (autophagosomes) that engulf damaged proteins, dysfunctional organelles, and misfolded components, then fuse with lysosomes for digestion and recycling. Works at the level of individual cells — cannot clear established multicellular tumor colonies which have already disabled these pathways.
First responders
NK cells — natural killer immune surveillance
Innate immune cells that continuously patrol for abnormal cells — virally infected, pre-cancerous, and cancerous. Kill on contact via perforin and granzyme without prior sensitisation. Activity is suppressed by cortisol, alcohol, poor sleep, and smoking. Activated by garlic allicin, exercise, vitamin D, and adequate sleep.
Adaptive killers
T cells — the targeted immune response
CD8+ cytotoxic T cells kill cancer cells via perforin/granzyme and Fas–FasL pathways. Require three signals: antigen recognition (TCR + MHC I), co-stimulation (CD28), and cytokines (IL-2, IL-12). Suppressed by PD-L1 which tumors express. Require zinc, omega-3, and vitamin D to develop and function optimally.
The kill switch
Apoptosis — programmed cell death
Triggered by DNA damage beyond repair, targeted therapy, and T-cell attack. Cancer cells disable their apoptosis pathways — overexpressing Bcl-2 and underexpressing p53 — which is why established tumor colonies cannot be cleared by the natural compounds and food interventions that work at the individual cell level.
Prevention vs treatment: Food, fasting, and supplements work at the level of individual cells — preventing damage and clearing pre-cancerous changes before they consolidate. They cannot clear established tumor colonies, which have already rewritten their own rules. That distinction is load-bearing.
Autophagy mechanisms — deep dive
What autophagy actually is
Autophagy (“self-eating”) is the cell’s recycling and quality-control system. It sequesters damaged proteins, dysfunctional organelles (especially mitochondria), and protein aggregates into double-membrane vesicles (autophagosomes), then fuses them with lysosomes for degradation. The resulting amino acids, fatty acids and nucleotides are returned to the cytosol for reuse. It operates at the level of individual cells.
Three main forms
Macroautophagy — bulk or selective engulfment by autophagosomes (the pathway most responsive to fasting and AMPK).
Microautophagy — direct invagination of the lysosomal membrane.
Chaperone-mediated autophagy (CMA) — one-by-one delivery of proteins carrying a KFERQ-like motif via HSC70 and LAMP2A.
Core sequence (macroautophagy)
1. Initiation
ULK1 complex
Nutrient/energy stress inhibits mTORC1 and activates AMPK. AMPK phosphorylates ULK1 (activating); mTORC1 normally keeps ULK1 inhibited. When mTOR drops, the ULK1–ATG13–FIP200–ATG101 complex translocates to the ER/omegasome and starts phagophore formation.
2. Nucleation
Beclin-1 / VPS34 complex
Class III PI3K (VPS34) with Beclin-1 generates PI3P on the isolation membrane. This recruits downstream effectors and expands the phagophore.
3. Elongation
ATG conjugation systems + LC3-II
Two ubiquitin-like systems (ATG12–ATG5–ATG16L1 and LC3 lipidation) expand the membrane. LC3-I is converted to membrane-bound LC3-II. Cargo adapters (p62/SQSTM1, NBR1, OPTN…) link specific damaged material to the growing autophagosome.
4. Fusion
Autolysosome formation
Completed autophagosome fuses with a lysosome (SNAREs, Rab7 and related machinery). Hydrolases then digest the contents.
5. Recycling
Building blocks returned
Amino acids, fatty acids, sugars and nucleotides are released back into the cytosol. Transcription factor TFEB simultaneously up-regulates lysosomal and autophagy genes when nutrients are low.
Decision logic the protocol uses
Nutrients high + energy high → mTORC1 ON → ULK1 inhibited → autophagy low
Nutrients low + energy low → AMPK ON, mTORC1 OFF → ULK1 active → autophagy high
The 16 h fast is timed to cross the point where AMPK rises and mTOR falls enough for Beclin-1/ULK1 activity to become meaningful. The fasted morning walk adds a second AMPK pulse while the cell is already nutrient-restricted. Food (especially amino acids and insulin) rapidly reactivates mTOR and suppresses the pathway — which is why the eating window is deliberate.
Boundaries (important)
Autophagy clears damaged or excess material inside individual cells. It does not clear established multicellular structures that have already disabled surveillance and apoptosis pathways. The system is homeostatic — both too little and uncontrolled excess can be harmful. Human evidence linking intermittent fasting or exercise to systemic outcomes is still developing; the molecular pathway itself is extensively mapped in cell and animal models.
The compounding stack
16h fast daily → mTOR suppressed, AMPK + autophagy active
Fasted morning walk → dual AMPK signal, lymphatic flush
Morning light → cortisol rhythm reset, circadian repair window set

Device & data integrations
BodyOS is a self-contained protocol app. It deliberately does not connect to Oura, WHOOP, Apple Health, Garmin or similar services yet.
Why not (for now)
• Wearable APIs (HealthKit, Health Connect, Oura/WHOOP OAuth) require platform-specific native code or a backend — this build is a pure web PWA that stays fully offline and private (all data in your browser).
• Full calorie / barcode / AI photo databases need large external services and change the product from a focused protocol tool into a general tracker.
• Social or coaching feeds add accounts, moderation and attention costs that conflict with the quiet daily-protocol design.

You can still log weight, energy, mood, sleep quality, meals and workouts manually — those feed the Stats trends. A future native or Capacitor build can add optional HealthKit / Health Connect sync without changing the core protocol.
